Cool-Season Comparison

Tall fescue usually beats Kentucky bluegrass for drought, but homeowners still search both

This is a classic comparison page because many homeowners are not really choosing between warm-season and cool-season yet. They are still trying to decide which cool-season direction holds up better when water pressure and summer stress get worse.

Direct Answer

For drought, tall fescue is usually the stronger choice than Kentucky bluegrass.

Bluegrass still has aesthetic appeal, but tall fescue is often the more realistic answer when summer resilience matters.

Why this comparison matters

Homeowners who still want a cool-season lawn often land here before they are ready to accept a warm-season shift. This page should help them understand why tall fescue stays in drought conversations so often.

Where bluegrass still attracts buyers

Kentucky bluegrass still carries appeal because of look and familiarity. That does not make it the stronger drought page, but it explains the search demand.

Where tall fescue wins

Tall fescue generally offers the better compromise once dry periods and summer stress become the deciding factor.
